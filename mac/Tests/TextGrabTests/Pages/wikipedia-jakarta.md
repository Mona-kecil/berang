# Jakarta

Coordinates: 6°11′S106°50′ECheckedFrom Wikipedia, the free encyclopediaFor other uses, see Jakarta (disambiguation).Jakarta,[a] officially the Special Capital Region of Jakarta,[b] is the capital andlargest city of Indonesia, with administrative status equivalent to a province. It lies on the northwestern coast of Java, bordering West Java and Banten and facing the Java Sea. Jakarta covers about 664 square kilometres (256 square miles) and had 11 million registered residents in 2025. Its urban area extends well beyond the provincial boundary into the wider Jakarta metropolitan area(locally known as Jabodetabek), which had a population of nearly 42 million in 2025 according to the United Nations.The area around present-day Jakarta was long associated with Sunda Kelapa, the port of the Sunda Kingdom. Islamic forces captured Kalapa in 1527, after which the settlement became known as Jayakarta and came under the Banten Sultanate. The Dutch East India Company (VOC) seized Jayakarta in 1619 and rebuilt it as Batavia, its main administrative centre in Asia and an important trading port. During the Japanese occupation in World War II, the authorities renamed Batavia to Jakarta. Indonesian independence was proclaimed there in 1945 and after the Indonesian National Revolution, Jakarta became the capital of the unitary republic in 1950.Jakarta grew rapidly after Indonesian independence. Migration from across Indonesia has shaped its population and culture. Urban growth has spread into Bogor,Depok, Tangerang and Bekasi, which creates a metropolitan region tied together by large, daily commuter flows. Jakarta is Indonesia's financial and trade centre and the centre of national administration. It is also home to many corporate headquarters, media organisations, universities and the secretariat of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN).Jakarta has no ethnic majority. The largest groups include Javanese, Betawi,Sundanese and Chinese Indonesians.Indonesian is the official language and the main language of public life, while Betawi culture developed from interaction among people of varying origins in colonial Batavia. Jakarta faces persistent issues including traffic congestion, flooding, land subsidence, air pollution, and pressure on water and waste services. Much of Jakarta lies on an alluvial plain, with elevations ranging from below sea level to about 50 metres (160 feet).[51][53] Thirteen rivers flow through the city from the south toward Jakarta Bay, including the Ciliwung, Angke, Sunter, and Grogolrivers.[51][53] The alluvial plain was historically swampy, and seasonal flooding remains common.[51] Heavy rain and runoff from upstream areas cause flooding, which can be worsened by high tides, land subsidence, blocked drains and limited river capacity.[54][55][56]Jakarta's flood-control policy has long favoured engineered measures, including canals, dredging, pumping stations and coastal defences.[57][58] Land subsidence is a major driver of coastal-flood risk in northern Jakarta.[59] Groundwater extraction and the weight of buildings and infrastructure are among the human causes of subsidence.[60][61] Beyond flooding, Jakarta also faces severe river-water and air pollution, with air pollution imposing substantial health burdens.[62]editedit sourceDrizzle in JakartaJakarta has a tropical monsoon climate with distinct wet and dry seasons.[63] Rainfall is much heavier from December to March, while June to September is relatively dry.[64] Flooding is common during the rainy season.
